# prosemirror-fragview

A fragmented view for ProseMirror. `FragmentedView` holds an ordinary `EditorState`, speaks the same protocol to the same plugins as `prosemirror-view`'s `EditorView`, and renders the document into a chain of FRAGMENTAINERS: components that pull content from a position and give back the position where they had to stop. The basic fragmentainer, `OverflowBox`, reports the browser's own overflow. A paginator's page is a fragmentainer that reports the best cut under its own rules, with every paged-media feature behind it, and still, in the end, a position.

The design and its phases are in [docs/plan.md](docs/plan.md).

Phases 1, 2, 3 and 4a are built. Phase 1 is the package, and the proof that a `FragmentedView` with one infinite `OverflowBox` behaves as an `EditorView`: 18 of 19 parity acts equal on document, selection, undo and redo, the nineteenth showing `EditorView` reading a stale selection under keys pressed back to back. Phase 2 is fixed-height boxes and the chain. The chain is held in plugin state and mapped through every transaction, and only dirty fragments are refilled, in order: one character typed refills one box and dirties none, a new line near the top ripples through all five boxes of the harness document, and undo restores the chain. A fragment the edit did not touch keeps its DOM, so a key costs about a box's worth of work whatever the document's length. The selection is painted across fragments as one highlight, geometry answers across the chain, a box shorter than its first line still takes it, a `near` window policy builds only the boxes around the viewport, and `moved` refills from late truth. Phase 3 is the slice renderer the paginator's CSS expects, measured on the studio's schema lab under its generic schema. Shallow siblings are put away by index, so `:nth-child` and `+` match in every fragment as in one rendering of the whole. A divided box carries the words of a continuation (`split`, `start`, `end`, `fragment`, `adjoin`), with the two sheets a host adopts. An opened `ol` continues its numbering through `value`: 13 of 13 markers equal at every cut. Structured marks are rebuilt from their instances and stamped with `data-txt-i`. Under 320 × 480 boxes the lab writes back with only its 6 known differences, every element a fragment shows is in the file or one of the parse's three repairs, and the generic-schema round's acts come out as expected, 8 of 8. Phase 4a opens the two doors a paginator's package needs of this one: the renderer's attribute and words are a registry, passed as `sliceRenderer(words)` or as the view's `words` prop, whose default is this package's own `data-fragview`, so a paginator brings its own spellings and the sweep that `src/` names no engine passes. And `invalidate()` releases every container and refills the chain from the first when the conditions of every fill change, a page size, a sheet or a late face, which the demo's page-size menu now calls instead of mounting a new view, with every count of phase 3 unchanged. What was built, the numbers and what is not done yet are in [docs/results.md](docs/results.md); `npm test`, `npm run parity`, `npm run chain`, `npm run lossless`, `npm run acts` and `npm run fragments` run it again. Written with BelType's studio as the venue. The first paginator to implement the fragmentainer is BelType's Cicero, in a package of its own, [prosemirror-beltype](https://codeberg.org/egildo/prosemirror-beltype), which imports this one and the engine; this package knows no engine, by the rule [*the engine knows no view*](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/main/.claude/decisions/project/the-engine-knows-no-view.md), and a sweep test over `src/` fails on the words `beltype` and `cicero`.
