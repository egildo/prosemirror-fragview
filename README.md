# prosemirror-fragview

A fragmented view for ProseMirror. `FragmentedView` holds an ordinary `EditorState`, speaks the same protocol to the same plugins as `prosemirror-view`'s `EditorView`, and renders the document into a chain of FRAGMENTAINERS: components that pull content from a position and give back the position where they had to stop. The basic fragmentainer, `OverflowBox`, reports the browser's own overflow. A paginator's page is a fragmentainer that reports the best cut under its own rules, with every paged-media feature behind it, and still, in the end, a position.

A fragmentainer's `fill(doc, from, render, hint)` is handed the hint the fragment before it answered (a named page, a blank page) on every fill, the factory's argument on the first and the latest on a refill, so a fragmentainer reads its hint there and keeps no copy of the one it was made with.

A fill answers where it stopped (`to`) and the hint for the next fragment, and may answer two more ranges, which the fragment's entry keeps and maps through every transaction: `reach`, the last position its decision read, and `shows`, the positions it shows outside its own range, as a page after a cut through a table row shows the rest of an earlier cell. A change in either dirties the fragment. A fill that rejects is answered by the `handleFillRejected` prop, `(view, error, container, from)`, or the entry stays dirty and the error is raised apart. Whatever is written into a page from outside the model, text or a whole element, is drawn over from the model, under a budget of three redraws per entry and one warning; a write inside a shadow root, or into a page during its own fill, is not seen.

The design and its phases are in [docs/plan.md](docs/plan.md).

Phases 1, 2, 3 and 4a are built. Phase 1 is the package, and the proof that a `FragmentedView` with one infinite `OverflowBox` behaves as an `EditorView`: 18 of 19 parity acts equal on document, selection, undo and redo, the nineteenth showing `EditorView` reading a stale selection under keys pressed back to back. Phase 2 is fixed-height boxes and the chain. The chain is held in plugin state and mapped through every transaction, and only dirty fragments are refilled, in order: one character typed refills one box and dirties none, a new line near the top ripples through all five boxes of the harness document, and undo restores the chain. A fragment the edit did not touch keeps its DOM, so a key costs about a box's worth of work whatever the document's length. The selection is painted across fragments as one highlight, geometry answers across the chain, a box shorter than its first line still takes it, a `near` window policy builds only the boxes around the viewport, and `moved` refills from late truth. Phase 3 is the slice renderer the paginator's CSS expects, measured on the studio's schema lab under its generic schema. Shallow siblings are put away by index, so `:nth-child` and `+` match in every fragment as in one rendering of the whole. A divided box carries the words of a continuation (`split`, `start`, `end`, `fragment`, `adjoin`), with the two sheets a host adopts. An opened `ol` continues its numbering through `value`: 13 of 13 markers equal at every cut. Structured marks are rebuilt from their instances and stamped with `data-txt-i`. Under 320 × 480 boxes the lab writes back with only its 6 known differences, every element a fragment shows is in the file or one of the parse's three repairs, and the generic-schema round's acts come out as expected, 8 of 8. Phase 4a opens the two doors a paginator's package needs of this one: the renderer's attribute and words are a registry, passed as `sliceRenderer(words)` or as the view's `words` prop, whose default is this package's own `data-fragview`, so a paginator brings its own spellings and the sweep that `src/` names no engine passes. And `invalidate()` releases every container and refills the chain from the first when the conditions of every fill change, a page size, a sheet or a late face, which the demo's page-size menu now calls instead of mounting a new view, with every count of phase 3 unchanged. What was built, the numbers and what is not done yet are in [docs/results.md](docs/results.md); `npm test`, `npm run parity`, `npm run chain`, `npm run lossless`, `npm run acts`, `npm run fragments` and `npm run compose` run it again. A composition is applied as the text it committed, at the selection held since `compositionstart`, and nothing is read back from the DOM ([results](docs/results.md#compositions-as-committed-text-the-results)). Written with BelType's studio as the venue. The first paginator to implement the fragmentainer is BelType's Cicero, in a package of its own, [prosemirror-beltype](https://codeberg.org/egildo/prosemirror-beltype), which imports this one and the engine; this package knows no engine, by the rule [*the engine knows no view*](https://github.com/egildo/beltype/blob/main/.claude/decisions/project/the-engine-knows-no-view.md), and a sweep test over `src/` fails on the words `beltype` and `cicero`.

## Browsers

Editing, selection and composition are verified in Chromium only: the harness and every suite run headless Chromium, and nothing here is measured in Firefox or WebKit. Elsewhere the view loads and works as an editor, unverified. Android is unsupported for now: there a composition over an existing word duplicates text in the document and Backspace skips the keymaps, so a host on Android should mount a plain `EditorView` instead. Where a browser sends a delete with no target range, the view takes one step of the model's: the character, the surrogate pair or the inline node beside the caret, or the selection.

## Node views

A leaf node type, or a block whose view has no content hole, may have a node view, through the `nodeViews` prop or a plugin's `props.nodeViews`, in `prosemirror-view`'s shape. The constructor receives `(node, view, getPos, decorations)`, where `decorations` are the node decorations whose range is exactly the node's, as `prosemirror-view` hands its outer decorations, and `getPos()` already answers the node's position while the constructor runs. A node view for text throws a `RangeError` at the prop. A view that answers a `contentDOM` is refused when it is made, its error raised apart while the node keeps its drawing, since a content hole would be divided across fragments.

The element is shown once, in the earliest page that holds the node whole. It keeps its state across refills and across a move to another page, and it leaves its page while the page fills. A block's view is placed only where the block stands whole in one page. A block the cut divides keeps `toDOM`'s drawing on every page that shows a piece, and the view warns once on the console, naming the type and position. The same holds for a leaf a page engine shows whole on two pages: the first has the face, the second its drawing. `update`, `destroy`, `selectNode`, `deselectNode`, `setSelection`, `stopEvent` and `ignoreMutation` mean what they mean in `prosemirror-view`; `multiType` is not read. `update(node, decorations)` is asked when the node changes and when its node decorations change, and a face that answers false is made again. `setSelection(anchor, head, root)` is asked when the model's selection lies inside a block's face, relative to the node's start; the view then writes no browser selection and marks nothing inside it. `getPos` answers `undefined` while the element is off its page.

The face follows the cut, and the cut never follows the face. To keep a fitting block whole, give it `break-inside: avoid` in the document's CSS, which moves it in the view and the print alike; a block taller than the page area divides in both.

`toDOM` still matters, for four reasons:

- Every measuring copy of the node, and the print, is drawn from it. The view pins a leaf's drawing to the element's size once it has one, so a leaf drawn at another size costs one more fill.
- A block's drawing is pinned to nothing, since a pinned table divided stretches the row before the cut past the page's foot. A block's view should draw at `toDOM`'s box; one that does not is warned once by its type and kept, and the cuts are made at `toDOM`'s size.
- A block leaf's `toDOM` should draw a block-level box, or the cuts differ from a print that has no wrapper.
- A node that shows words should show the same words in both, or a guard comparing the pages with the print will refuse.

In every copy the node stands in a wrapper marked `data-fragview-face`, which is what `nodeDOM` answers; a block's wrapper holds its whole drawing, its content mapped as without the wrapper. The wrapper that holds the element carries `data-fragview-face="placed"`, and one that holds a drawing keeps the empty value, so a host reads `view.nodeDOM(pos)?.getAttribute('data-fragview-face')` to know whether the element is shown, and CSS can dress a divided drawing. A CSS selector on the node's siblings or parent sees the wrapper. For a divided block, a host may position its own element over the drawing from a plugin view, reading `nodeDOM`; nothing here helps or hinders that.
